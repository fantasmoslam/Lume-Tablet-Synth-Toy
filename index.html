Lume is a crossed-ribbon chord machine. The chord bank on the left is strummed by the right ribbon; the note bank on the right gets a melody harp on the left ribbon. Everything stays in the key and scale you pick, so there are no wrong notes — only choices. This manual follows what you do, not where the buttons live: play the banks, shape a sound, sequence and play pads, record, then connect.

Quick start

  1. Tap the screen (or press any key) to switch on. LUME lights up for a few seconds, then the display settles into a resting animation. Headphones recommended.
  2. Tap a chord on the left bank. It latches and stays lit; tap it again to let go.
  3. Swipe the right ribbon up or down to strum it. With no chord held it strums the last chord you played (or the I chord). Faster swipes are louder and tighter.
  4. Play the right bank for single notes, and sweep the left ribbon for a melody over them.
  5. Press PLAY (or T) for the beat, then tap the sixteen step pads to write chord steps.
  6. Tap a tab above the step pads — CHORD, MELODY, SPACE and the rest — to shape a sound without leaving the keys. Every tab has a MORE button that opens the full sheet.

Playing the key banks

The two key banks are the heart of the instrument, and both are always in your chosen key and scale.

  • Chords latch like Omnichord buttons: tap to hold (the key stays lit), tap again to release. With the Chords HOLD switch off — the switch beside the CHORD tab, and its light — a chord sounds only while your finger is down.
  • Notes are momentary unless the Notes HOLD switch (beside the MELODY tab) or the H key turns hold on. Both stay in step. Turning hold off releases held notes only; the drone and chord keep playing.
  • Octave: every instrument has its own octave, from −3 to +3, set with − / + on its panel (OCTAVE) or in its sheet. On a keyboard, , and . shift the note bank.
  • Strum / slide: a quick swipe across a bank plays every key it crosses, in order, in either direction; a slow drag does not retrigger, so you can move a finger with care. Each bank's sheet has a Slide control with three settings: Strum (the default), Off (one finger plays one key, and several fingers work at once) and Glide (every key your finger touches plays, however slowly).
  • Voicing: the pill's Voicing key cycles the chords through Triad → 7th → 9th → Sus4. All Off silences everything that is sounding (notes, chords, drone, ribbons, keyboard, theremin) and re-centres the pitch bend. Arp Hold keeps the arpeggiator running on the last chord after you let go.
The ribbons

Tap a ribbon for one harp strike. Height is pitch across four octaves, snapped to the scale on the left ribbon, or to the latched chord on the right.

  • Sweep up or down for a glissando; sweep speed sets your velocity and how tight the strum is.
  • Damper: touch the bottom of the ribbon, or use a second finger, to kill the tail. The chord itself keeps playing.
  • Settings: open the Rib L or Rib R tab (or that tab's MORE). Each ribbon has its own voice, Level, Tone, Tail, Sub and Vibrato, plus presets, octave, envelope and effects like any other instrument.
  • Sideways & Dwell: in a ribbon's sheet, choose what a sideways slide and a still hold do — Off, Filter, Pitch, Shimmer, Reverb or Echo. Dwell swells while your finger rests and drains when you move. Touch sets how far each reaches.
  • Two hands: with both ribbons down, the higher hand brightens and the lower darkens — a subtle split on top of each side's own Tone.
The stage panels (the tab strip)

Above the step pads is a strip of tabs. Tapping one drops that instrument's controls into the sequencer's strip while the key banks, ribbons and master knobs stay fully playable — so shaping a sound never costs you the ability to play.

  • The tabs, left to right: RIB·L · MELODY · DRONE · SEQ · PADS · SPACE · THEREMIN · CHORD · RIB·R.
  • Tap a tab to open its panel; tap it again (or the ✕ at either corner) to close. SEQ returns to the sequencer strip.
  • Each panel ends in MORE, which opens the full sheet — the same tab in the big dialog — where every control lives.
  • They are crossed on purpose: the melody (right-hand) instrument's controls sit on the left side, and the chord (left-hand) instrument's controls on the right, so the hand that is playing never has to leave its bank — the same logic as the crossed ribbons.
  • The tab labels move with you: each shows its instrument's current voice, so you always know what you are about to shape.
LFO (movement) and the Theremin panel

Every bank and ribbon panel has an LFO ▸ button, captioned MOVEMENT. Its light pulses at the LFO's speed and it shows the rate and target, or OFF. Tap it for the LFO page: RATE (one sweep every 20 seconds up to vibrato speed), DEPTH (0 = off), TARGET (filter, pitch or pan) and SHAPE (sine, triangle, square, saw). ◂ SOUND goes back. The Theremin panel shows what it hears (NOW PLAYING) and adds RANGE (how far one hand-sweep bends), GLIDE (smoother vs. snappier tracking), LEVEL, ECHO, OCTAVE, and IN KEY, which snaps the pitch to your key's notes. Every panel button has a caption underneath, and tapping the open tab (✕) closes its panel.

Playing live

The performance module (beside DRONE) has four switches. FX OFF mutes the echo and reverb returns for a dry moment. FREEZE holds the current reverb and echo tails while new playing stays dry; tap again to let go. UNDO steps back through your last sound changes (knobs, voices, effects), up to 30, and shows how many are waiting. LOCK: hold it for half a second to lock; hold again to unlock. While locked, the menus, sheet, tab strip panels, tempo, voicing, step pads and recorder ignore taps (LOCK flashes to remind you), but the key banks, ribbons, master knobs, ARP, ARP HOLD, SLOT, PLAY, the HOLD switches, DRONE and ALL OFF all stay live.

Slots: while the sequencer plays, a slot change waits for the next bar, and the SLOT button blinks with where it's going (Slot A→C). Tap SLOT for the next one; hold SLOT to pick A, B, C or D directly. In Bank you can choose whether the tempo jumps or glides to the new slot over one bar. The four lights under the LUME display count the beats (red on the downbeat).

Mix: LOW CUT on the master row trims rumble and mud for the room (it stays put when you change slots). The echo and reverb returns always have a gentle low cut. A limiter sits just before the output; the meter's LIMIT light turns amber when it catches peaks and red at the ceiling.

Count-in (Bank → Start): PLAY gives one bar of clicks first, with the beat lights counting in white; tap PLAY again to cancel. The tape recorder counts in too, or, if the sequencer is already running, starts exactly on the next bar. Stage mode (Look → Performance) turns the body dark for dark stages and brightens the display and lights.

Backup (top of the Bank tab): Export saves one file with everything Lume keeps on this device: banks, slots, sounds, DX7 voices and settings. Import restores it here or on another device (it replaces what's there, then Lume restarts). Export before clearing browser data or reinstalling.

MIDI: set Clock in to Follow and Lume takes its tempo, PLAY and STOP from the connected device or DAW. Program Change 1–4 selects slots A–D.

The sheet (MORE)

Every panel's MORE button opens the sheet: the full settings for that instrument. Its tabs follow the tab strip's order, with Bank, MIDI, Look and Manual set apart on the right. Each instrument's settings sit in labelled wells (Preset & Play, LFO, Envelope, Filter & Effects, Voice), using the same knobs as the panels; drag a knob up or down, and its value shows underneath. The Global strip holds the key and scale for the whole instrument. The voice library scrolls, and DX7 marks the FM voices. PLAY BANKS shrinks the sheet so both key banks stay playable underneath while you tweak; tap it again for the full sheet. ✕ closes it.

ENV, glide and touch dynamics

The Melody and Chord panels have an ENV ▸ page: ATTACK, DECAY, SUSTAIN and RELEASE, drawn live as a shape. SOURCE switches between the voice's own envelope (VOICE) and yours (CUSTOM); turning any envelope knob switches to CUSTOM. VELOCITY sets how much touch matters: the key banks read how hard you land (finger contact size, S Pen pressure, or a MIDI controller's velocity), and at 0 every note plays the same. On the melody, GLIDE slides each new note up or down from the last one (OFF at 0). The LFO page's SYNC locks its speed to the tempo, from 4 bars down to 1/16. Double-tap any knob to return it to its default.

Voices & the sound menus

Lume has four instruments — the chord bank, the note bank, the left ribbon and the right ribbon — plus the drone voice and the theremin. Each has a full sheet: open it from a tab's MORE button or, for the banks and ribbons, from the big dialog's Chord / Melody / Rib L / Rib R tabs.

Voices

  • 27 built-in voices (Poly, EPiano, Organ, Strings, Brass, Harp, GlassHarp, Celeste, Luna and more), plus any FM · DX7 banks you load.
  • Voice browser: choose a source (All / Built-in / any loaded bank), filter by type (Keys, Bells, Pluck, Pads, Strings, Organ, Brass, Bass, Lead, FX), then page through the grid with ◀ ▶ or by swiping it. Imported voices are sorted into types automatically.
  • FM · DX7: Lume has a 6-operator, 32-algorithm FM engine. Press Load .syx to import a DX7 bank (32 voices) or a single voice; 4-operator banks (TX81Z, DX21…) are not supported. Loaded banks are remembered on this device and can be removed from the browser.

Sound shaping

  • Presets: pick a factory preset, or Save your own under a name. Del removes one of yours. Presets are kept on this device.
  • Octave: shift that instrument alone by up to three octaves either way.
  • Envelope (ADSR): Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release, with a small graph. The button reads Voice while the voice uses its own envelope; move any slider to switch to Custom, and press it to go back.
  • Effects: Filter and Reso shape the tone — on the chord and note banks this is a resonant filter that can growl and, pushed far enough, sing a pitch of its own. Drive adds grit, Chorus thickens, and Echo / Reverb send that instrument to the shared tape echo and shimmer reverb. Unison and Width stack and spread detuned copies.
  • Modulation: a slow mover per instrument — Mod rate, Mod depth and a Mod target (Filter, Pitch or Pan). This is the control to reach for to make a held chord or drone breathe instead of sitting flat.
The arpeggiator

The arp plays the notes of the chord you are holding, one after another. Turn it on with the pill's Arp key or G, and set its speed with Rate − and Rate +. The full controls sit at the top of the Chord sheet.

  • Arp: on or off, the same as the pill key.
  • Hold: keeps arpeggiating the last chord after you let go, so both hands are free.
  • Sync: locks the rate to the tempo, so 1/16 always means a sixteenth note. Turn it off for fixed speeds.
  • Rate: 1/8, 1/8 triplet, 1/16, 1/16 triplet or 1/32.
  • Pattern: Up, Down, Up-Dn or Random. Octaves: 1–4. Gate: how long each note sounds, from short and detached to fully connected.
The drone

The drone is a second, independent sustained chord that layers underneath normal play. Start and stop it with the Drone switch near the top of the instrument (its LED lights and shows the chord it holds), the DRONE tab, or the readout in the display. Keep tapping other chords, latch and release them, even change key — the drone plays on underneath until you stop it.

  • Voicing: Chord, Fifths, Octaves or Sus2. Follow chords makes the drone track whatever chord you are playing.
  • Tone & motion: Sub octave, Tone, Breath, Breath rate, Warble and Pulse (with a division from 1/2 to 1/16).
  • Air: Wind and Crackle — a slow noise bed and sparse vinyl dust.
  • Grain: a filtered-noise bed (Level and Tone). Sparkle: sparse bell-like events (Rate, Level and Ring length), always sent generously to the delay and reverb so they trail off into the distance. Grain and Sparkle stop when the drone does.
  • A fresh drone with no modulation set yet gets a gentle default movement and texture, so it is alive from the first note rather than sitting static.
Space: shimmer reverb & tape echo

Open the Space tab for the shared ambience every instrument sends to, and use the Space master knob up top for a quick hand on the reverb.

  • Shimmer: on/off and Amount — a pitch-shifted layer inside the reverb that feeds back an octave up on every pass.
  • Reverb: Decay, Tone and Mix — a long, smooth plate built from a feedback delay network.
  • Tape echo: Level, Time and Repeats — a pitch-warped delay with wow and flutter.
  • Tape hiss: the faint analog noise floor. Reset returns the whole Space section to its defaults.
The sequencer

Two lanes run on one clock. Any beat works with any progression, with your own playing on top.

Chord lane

  • With REC lit and the sequencer stopped, chord taps sketch steps.
  • While it plays, right-ribbon strums are overdubbed with their real velocity and direction.
  • On a step pad, a short tap fills it with the last chord or clears it. A long press ties it for 1, 2 or 4 sixteenths. A ↓ marks a down-strum, and a dim pad is soft.
  • CLR wipes this lane only.

Beat lane

  • The lane key beside CLR cycles what the sixteen pads edit: CH (chords), then K kick, S snare, H hat and R rim. The same picker is on the Rib R tab.
  • Each pad has four tiny LEDs (kick, snare, hat, rim). Amber means that drum hits there, and a white outline marks the track you are editing.
  • With a drum lane selected, tapping a pad toggles that hit and plays a preview.
  • The Rib R tab also has Clr beat (wipe the kit), Beat off (mute drums, chords keep playing), Tap tempo and the chord morph buttons.

Tempo & feel

  • Tempo runs 30–240, swing 0–60% and human 0–100% (a little timing and velocity jitter so a programmed pattern does not sound machine-quantised). Swing, human and lane live on the Rib R tab.
  • In the pill, use − and + for one BPM at a time (hold to speed up), drag the number up or down, or tap the number to type an exact BPM on a keypad.
  • TAP averages your last nine taps and ignores stray ones. Tap a rough tempo, then fine-tune with − and +.
The PAD bank

Open the PADS tab for a bank of thirty assignable pads — two rows of fifteen — across four pages (A–D), so 120 in all. They sit in the sequencer's strip while the keys stay live.

  • Tap a pad to play it. Chords and notes sound for as long as you hold the pad, just like keys; drums fire a hit.
  • Press EDIT, then tap a pad to assign it: choose its type (Chord, Note, Drum or Off), then the chord degree, the note and octave, or the drum — and optionally a voice from the whole library. Changes apply and sound at once.
  • PAGE cycles A–D; the four dots show where you are. Pads are remembered on this device.
Recording

The recorder is the tape deck on the left of the master row. Press the red button to record everything reaching the speakers (including Space), and press it again to stop.

  • The tape window draws your take as it is captured, and zooms out when the window fills.
  • Transport: ⏮ back to start, ▶ play/pause, ⟲ loop and ⤓ download.
  • Tap the tape to jump there and play, or drag it to scrub with sound, like rocking a tape past the head.
  • Short and medium takes download as a universally openable WAV; takes over ten minutes stay compressed, to keep the device happy.
The output section
  • Volume, Filter, Drive and Space are master knobs across the top of the instrument: overall level, a resonant master filter sweep, overall grit, and overall reverb amount. The display's first line opens Key, Scale and master Volume as well.
  • The output meter beside them is a true stereo peak meter of what actually leaves Lume, after every effect and the safety limiter — fast bars, a white peak-hold tick, and a numeric dB readout of the loudest moment in the last second or so.
  • The PEAK light goes amber when the limiter is gently working and red at the ceiling. Tap it to reset the holds.
Bank & songs
  • Four slots A–D, each holding a complete setup: chord steps, drum kit, tempo, swing and the sound. The pill's Slot key cycles them live, mid-beat, like flipping a stompbox preset.
  • The Bank tab — which the pill's Menu button opens — shows all four: go switches, copy duplicates into the next slot, ✕ wipes. It also holds the pattern libraries: Beats load the drum lane only and Progressions load the chord lane only, so the other lane stays untouched.
  • Everything you edit is autosaved on this device. Because the sound is part of a slot, switching slots recalls its patch too.
  • Type a Song order like AABC and PLAY walks those slots, one bar each, looping.

Theremin (camera)

Lume can play the drone hands-free from your webcam, theremin-style. Open the Theremin tab and press ON — only that switch touches the camera, so a stray tap cannot trigger a permission prompt.

  • An open hand starts the drone; a fist stops it. A little hysteresis keeps it from flickering at the threshold.
  • Hand distance from the camera bends the drone's pitch, up to an octave either way.
  • Hand height sets the chord bank's filter.
  • Choose the theremin's own voice, Reso and Reverb here. Lume snapshots the chord patch when you start, forces a clean theremin baseline, and puts your patch back when you stop.

The hand tracker is loaded from the network the first time you switch the theremin on, so its first use needs a connection.

The display

Text lines

  1. Key, scale, any moved octaves and the MIDI link. Tap this line (▾) for the quick key picker: a small piano for the key and six scale buttons. It changes as you tap, the drone follows, and you can play the banks while it is open.
  2. What is running: drone, arp, sequence and BPM (READY when nothing is).
  3. Current chord (with 7, 9 or sus suffix) and the notes you are holding.
  4. Bend, the last strum velocity and morph.

Dot-matrix wordmark

LUME stays lit for a few seconds after power-on, then its dots fade out one by one and a resting animation takes over — drifting particles, rain, twinkling stars or a slow wave. It is picked at random each start and changes every minute or two.

Everything you play moves it: a chord sends a ripple, a strum a sweep, a note a rising blip and a drum hit a scatter of sparks. Devices set to reduce motion keep the static wordmark.

Sound design notes

Deliberately analog-flavoured: soft tube drive → stereo chorus → a warmth filter and faint hiss → a pitch-warped tape delay → a big, smooth shimmer reverb.

  • Push a ribbon's Tail past 80% with GlassHarp or Luna for cosmic harp ambience.
  • Drop the tail and set swing near 25% for lo-fi.
  • Sub adds an octave shimmer line; Unison and Width thicken a voice; Modulation aimed at Filter is the quickest way to make a held chord breathe.
Looks & performance

Open the Look tab to change how Lume is dressed. Your pick is remembered, and it changes only the appearance: sound, patterns and MIDI stay as they are.

  • Lume is the original bone-and-orange panel.
  • Famicom, NES, SNES and Genesis borrow each console's shell and button colours; the display and wordmark recolour to match.
  • Retro Wave is neon pink and cyan over a purple sunset grid. Clear Shell is a see-through case over a glowing green circuit board. Nostromo is a deep-space freighter console: putty plastic, hazard yellow and green phosphor. Pocket is a gray brick with an olive-green screen. Blueprint is white ink on drafting-paper blue. CT-101 is a satin black panel over walnut, with ivory keys and a warm amber glow.

Performance

  • Animation (Full / Light / Off) sets how hard the display works; Off keeps the static wordmark. Audio buffer (Stable / Balanced / Fast) trades responsiveness against crackle — reload to apply.
Install & devices
  • Lume needs no internet once it is open (except the camera theremin's first use).
  • To install it like an app, open the hosted copy in Chrome (an address starting with https://). On Android, use the browser menu → Install app, or the Install Lume button at the bottom of this tab when Chrome offers it. On Windows or Mac, use the install icon at the right end of the address bar. It then opens full screen from its own icon and works offline.
  • A tablet is the sweet spot. Phones are cramped but playable in landscape.
